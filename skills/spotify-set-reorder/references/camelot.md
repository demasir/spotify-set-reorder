# Camelot wheel — harmonic mixing reference

Used in DJ Set mode for planning transitions. Vibe Curation mode ignores this entirely.

## The wheel

| Camelot | Minor key | Major key | (Camelot) |
|---------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| 1A      | A♭ minor  | B major   | 1B        |
| 2A      | E♭ minor  | F♯ major  | 2B        |
| 3A      | B♭ minor  | D♭ major  | 3B        |
| 4A      | F minor   | A♭ major  | 4B        |
| 5A      | C minor   | E♭ major  | 5B        |
| 6A      | G minor   | B♭ major  | 6B        |
| 7A      | D minor   | F major   | 7B        |
| 8A      | A minor   | C major   | 8B        |
| 9A      | E minor   | G major   | 9B        |
| 10A     | B minor   | D major   | 10B       |
| 11A     | F♯ minor  | A major   | 11B       |
| 12A     | C♯ minor  | E major   | 12B       |

A = minor side. B = major side.

## Transition quality ranking

From cleanest to most jarring:

1. **Same key (same number + same letter)** — e.g. 8A → 8A. Perfect blend, no key change perceived.
2. **Switch letter (same number)** — e.g. 8A ↔ 8B. Relative major/minor switch. Very clean, brightens or darkens without dissonance.
3. **±1 number, same letter** — e.g. 7A ↔ 8A. The "energy boost" move; shifts up/down a fifth in the circle. Standard pro move.
4. **±1 number AND switch letter** — e.g. 8A ↔ 7B. Moderate jump. Mitigable with stems/EQ during a long blend.
5. **±2 or more steps** — increasingly disruptive. Requires explicit technique: drop one element on filter while introducing the other, breakdown bridges, or cold drop (intentional dramatic shift).

## When to break the rules

Camelot-clean is a default, not a religion. Pro DJs break it intentionally to:
- Create a "wow" moment with a key change that lands on a drop
- Hand off energy to another DJ where a cold drop signals the handoff
- Carry a specific track that's too good to skip even if it's harmonically isolated

If the skill makes a jump >1 step, it must declare the mitigation technique in the transition note.

## Practical priorities

1. BPM-compatible (within ±2-3 BPM typically; pitching tracks more changes their feel)
2. Camelot-clean if available
3. Cultural / arc fit — sometimes a "lesser" Camelot pair is the right narrative choice

Order of priority varies by style. Tech house tolerates harder jumps (heavy percussion masks them). Melodic techno is much less forgiving — the harmonic content is the point.
